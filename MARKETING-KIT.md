**What the edit does:** pauses longer than 0.4 s are shortened (measured on the audio, so no word is clipped), the framing punches in at each cut and each new section, key lines become moving type, captions appear word by word, her voice is cleaned and levelled to −14 LUFS (Instagram/YouTube loudness), and a soft reversed-whoosh swell and a quiet tick (ElevenLabs sound effects) mark the type moments.

### Reel 1 · Taught to hide

In a lot of Indian homes, anger had one meaning: disrespect. Raise your voice and you were rude. Push back and you were difficult. "This hurt me", said to an elder, was out of the question.

So we learned early to swallow it, and to tell ourselves it wasn't a big deal. Some of us got so good at it we started calling it maturity.

But anger was never the problem. It's information.

### Reel 2 · Anger is information

Anger is not the problem. Anger is information.

It tells you when a boundary has been crossed. When something matters to you deeply. When you've been treated in a way that doesn't sit right.

Feeling it doesn't make you a bad person. It makes you honest. The goal was never to stop feeling angry — it's to feel it, name it, and say it honestly.

## Two quick checks before posting

1. **Hindi subtitles** (`.hi.srt`) were translated by me — please have Priyanka (or another Hindi speaker) read them once.
2. **One word at about 1:09 of the full talk.** Just before "Feeling it does not make you a bad person" there is a short syllable. Four transcriptions: Whisper (full file) "Feeling"; Whisper (that second alone) "No, feeling"; ElevenLabs Scribe v1 and v2 "Not feeling". "Not feeling it doesn't make you a bad person" would contradict her next line ("It makes you honest"), so the captions read **"Feeling it doesn't make you a bad person"**. Please listen once and tell me if she meant something else.
