Twenty-nine directions across six rounds, each shown large and then at the sizes a favicon and an app icon are actually used at. Rendered from the real SVGs — nothing here is a mockup, and nothing is wired into the site.

Round six — Indian visual tradition, used as structure

Every full mark in this round fails at 32 and 16, and that is not a defect of these five: Indian visual systems are systems, and systems need room. Each earns its place on the large mark plus a separately drawn icon. None is a mark you can simply shrink. The brief's named risk was drifting spa-ward — one direction did, and was killed before it became a candidate.

Z2 — Jaali

A pierced screen in the plain octagon-and-square cut — a real geometric jaali, not floral arabesque. Every aperture identical; one has the light behind it.

Its pick, and the only one of five whose icon cut is as strong as the full mark — razor-sharp at 16px, dark ground and one aperture, no ambiguity. A jaali is a privacy device before a decorative one, cut so someone could look out without being looked at: that is confidentiality, a clinical obligation rather than a mood. It also carries saṃvṛti almost literally — a surface you see through but not clearly. It avoided the overlapping-circle jaali deliberately, because that one produces six-petal flowers and goes straight to spa.

Not freehanded and not evoked — constructed. Also a drawing made daily, at a doorstep, by one person, before anyone else is up.

Round five — layered, with something concealed

Each of these reads as one thing and then as another. Look at the large version for a few seconds before reading the caption — if the second layer only appears once you have been told about it, it isn't there, and that is the test these were built to pass or fail.

Its pick. Schröder's stairs, 1858 — a genuine bistable figure, so the mind content is perceptual psychology rather than anatomy. The same steps are a climb or a ceiling depending on where you stand, and nothing on the page changed: that is reframing, drawn. Chosen partly because the flip is involuntary — unlike an illusory contour, which some people simply don't see. Best 16px behaviour in the round. Risk: reads as a plain stairs logo to anyone who doesn't linger.

The strongest piece of design in the round and the one most native to the name. Every thread's displacement is the exact height of a hidden hemisphere at that point, so the object is geometrically precise and entirely absent — the mark is the appearance, and the thing itself is not in the file. A Kanizsa illusory contour, and the sharpest thinking in the round: five parts, no whole, but you cannot stop seeing the whole. Round four — researched against 144 real therapy logos

Before drawing, this round scraped and counted the motifs in 144 real counselling and psychotherapy marks: trees ~16, brains ~9, lotus ~8, head-in-profile ~7, birds ~7, sunrises ~6. Zero doors, windows, stairs, margins, punctuation, plumbs, weaving or water levels. These six come from that empty column.
