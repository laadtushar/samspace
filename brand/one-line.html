One line

The two you chose, and ten drawn to sit beside them — five that share their armature, then five more where each takes a different object. Every one is shown large, then at the sizes a favicon is actually used at, with its separately drawn reduction.

What makes these a family

  • One path, one M, no Z — genuinely one unbroken line, never closed. Checked against every file, not taken on trust.
  • A coil that tightens, or a deliberate refusal of one. It is the strongest gesture the two you picked have in common.
  • The line does two jobs — in K the contour becomes the interior; in J the tangle and the spiral are the same thread.
  • Emptiness carries the mark. Most of each one is bare ground.
  • One stroke, so one colour. None of the five took clay: a continuous line cannot change colour partway without becoming two drawings. Warmth here would have to come from the ground, not the line.

J — The Thread your pick

A loose tangle lets one thread go; it crosses a wide empty middle and winds into a close, still spiral. Nothing is cut and nothing added — the same length of thread, wound.

JK5 — The Long Question

It begins as a ruled line — the line you write on — and runs level and almost empty across three quarters of the mark; then it lifts off the rule into the hook of a question. Where a question mark puts a separate dot, this line does not lift: it carries on down and winds into the coil.

Its pick. The only one of the five that survives 16px with its idea intact, the only one that speaks to the academic half of the practice, and its construction move — the dot that is not a dot, because the line never lifted — is exactly K's move transposed. It also gives you an armature nothing else does: a rule a wordmark can sit on. Caveat it raised itself: the most literal device in the set, and the one that risks reading as stock punctuation.

JK1 — Between Two

Two people facing each other, drawn without lifting. The line runs down the first face to the throat, carries on across the gap and rises as the second figure's throat — so the second person is the first person's own line continued. Then it leaves both bodies, crosses a bare field, and winds into a spiral belonging to neither.

The best mark at full size, and its icon cannot carry the idea. It tried five icon layouts; in every one the two noses merge and the pair reads as a single animal head with ears. The shipped icon drops the first figure entirely — which costs the direction its whole point at favicon size and leaves the silhouette close to K's. A fair trade if the practice is mostly seen at header size; not if the favicon matters.

JK2 — The Unwinding

The family gesture run backwards. It starts at the dead centre of a tight coil and every turn is wider than the last, so the spiral accelerates instead of settling; the final turn does not close, it leaves, and the thread runs away and simply stops on a bare open end.

Best-behaved of the five at 32px. Two caveats, both its own: it sits close to M-loose-end, which is also “released rather than wound”; and read cold it can look like a decorative flourish, because we read left to right — tail into centre — which is winding.

JK3 — Not Yet Pulled

A thread comes in bare, crosses itself three times — an overhand knot, drawn open, never pulled — and runs straight out the other side into the coil. The crossings are the subject: not a tangle, three decisions. It can still be undone from either end.

The most useful failure in the round. Three crossings at 32px with a heavy stroke is not achievable while keeping the tails: open it enough and two crossings vanish and it reads as a fish; keep it dense and the lobes fill solid. The shipped icon gives up the spiral and the runs and keeps only the knot — which holds all three crossings, and reads as a heraldic trefoil. The idea and the icon pull in opposite directions.

JK4 — Through

The line runs in level, dips, and winds inward to a tight centre — then, without reversing and without lifting, keeps turning the same way while the radius grows, comes back out between its own turns, crosses its own rim and goes on, a little lower than it came in.

Its own drop, and the most original idea of the five. The coil is in the middle of the line, not at its end: the tight place is somewhere the thread went through, not somewhere it stayed. Dropped because beside JK2 it is the same picture at a glance. Its first version read unmistakably as a breaking wave until the tangents were solved so the thread enters and leaves level.

JK6 — The Whorl

One unbroken line draws a thumb held to the page: up the left edge of the pad, over the tip, down the right — and low on the side where a real print has its delta the edge stops being an edge and becomes a ridge, turning back on itself and winding inward three and a half times to a still centre. The contour and the print are the same stroke. In India a thumb impression is what you leave when a signature will not do: the plainest available proof that a particular person was here.

The strongest full mark of the five, and the icon changes the subject. At size the thumb and the ridge are unmistakable and the construction is exactly K's move — contour becoming interior — done on a different body part. Cut to 16px the pad and the delta collapse into a curl inside a bowl and it reads as an @. Whether that is a defect is your call: samvriti.space is an address, and an @ is not an accident anyone would have to explain. It is also the only mark here that touches identity and consent rather than metaphor.

JK7 — The Glass

One unbroken line draws a cutting-chai glass and what comes off it. It starts as a loose lip at the left rim, goes down the wall, across the base and up the far wall — and at the rim it does not stop: the same stroke lifts off as steam, wavers twice, and winds into a close, still coil above the glass. The vessel and the thing it gives off are one line, and the only part of it that holds anything is open at the top.

The warmest and the least serious. The full mark is the only one on the sheet that says something true about the actual format — an hour at home, on a screen, with something hot going cold beside the laptop. Two caveats, both real: the steam-and-coil is the third variation of J's gesture on this sheet, and the icon nearly became a seated figure, which would have collided head-on with JK1. What shipped instead — a squat vessel with a ball floating off one side — is legible as a vessel and not much more.

JK8 — The Pin

One unbroken length of wire. It begins as the point, runs down the long shaft, wraps once and a half into the spring at the foot, comes back up the other arm and ends by closing the cap over the point it started as — near it, not touching it. This is the one sibling that refuses the family spiral: the turn at the foot does not tighten to a still centre. It is a working coil, held under tension, and the tension is the only reason the thing holds together at all.

A stated refusal of the family rule, and the only one carrying freight from outside the brand. As drawing it is the cleanest icon of the five: a safety pin survives 16px because everyone already knows the silhouette. But since 2016 a worn safety pin has been a political solidarity badge in the US and UK, and a therapy practice serving students abroad would be adopting that reading whether or not it meant to. Include it because you should see the refusal argued, not because it is safe.

JK9 — The Kite

One unbroken line, running the other way from the rest of the family. It begins at the still centre of the wound thread, unwinds two and a third turns off the reel, crosses a wide empty field as a slack string — and where it reaches the kite it neither stops nor becomes something else: it goes straight on up through the middle as the spine, then out around the four edges and back. The thing holding the kite down and the thing holding the kite up are one length of thread.

Its pick, and the only icon of the five that survives 16px with its idea intact. A diamond with a spine and a loop off one corner is still a kite at any size. The direction it runs — out of the coil rather than into it, release rather than settling — is a genuine alternative reading of the practice, and it inverts JK2's move without repeating its picture. Caveat: a kite reads young. For the student half of the practice that is probably right; for adult clients it may read light.

JK10 — The Point

One unbroken line draws a pencil and the mark it has just made, and they are the same line. It starts short of the tip on one side of the sharpened cone, runs up that edge and the length of the shaft, across the blunt end, back down to the point — and out of the point it keeps going as graphite, runs a little way clear, and winds into a close, still coil. The instrument and its trace are one stroke.

Its own drop. The idea is the best argument for the mentoring half of the practice — the tool is not separate from what it leaves behind — and the full mark carries it. The icon does not: at 34 the shaft reads as a handle and the open curl as a bowl, so it reads first as a spoon, then as a crochet hook. It is here because the full-size lockup is genuinely usable beside a wordmark, not because the reduction works.

On the first five. Seen stacked, four of them sit on the same armature — a long horizontal run with an event on it — which is J's armature, so they are legitimately in-family, but it made the sheet more repetitive than J and K alone.

On the second five. That is what the round below was for: five different objects, and three different frames — two upright (JK6, JK8), one square (JK10), two landscape. All ten still pass the same mechanical check as J and K: one <path>, one M, zero Z, stroke 6.5 at full size, only the two brand colours.

The brain was attempted and is not here. Five constructions were drawn and rendered: a modulated spiral read as a rosette; a bean outline with an interior spiral read as a snail; an anatomical outline read as an ammonite; a dense spiral read as a chrysanthemum; a serpentine read as nothing at all. A brain is legible only from a closed bumpy contour plus a second mark for the fissure — and this family forbids both the closing and the second mark. Brain plus terminal coil is a snail, near enough to a law. If you want one, it needs a stated two-stroke exception, and that is a decision about the family rule, not a drawing problem.
