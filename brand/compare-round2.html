Round two — five different kinds of thing

Deliberately not four variations on one idea this time: a wordmark, a bilingual lockup, a repeating pattern, a textured overprint and a drawing. Each keeps its own proportions — squaring them up would make them different marks. Ordered strongest to weakest.

E — Wordmark

Lettering only, no symbol. The practice is called .space, so the wordmark is made of space — the tracking is the mark. Three clay dots (two i-tittles and the period) are the only warm things in it.

No icon reduction at all: 4:1 at 32px is a smear, and a cropped square greys out. Would need a separate monogram.

F — Bilingual shirorekha

Latin stands on a baseline; Devanagari hangs from the shirorekha. One clay rule does both jobs and runs past both words. The anusvara and the matra arm rising above the line is the best detail in the set.

I — Kantha

Not an icon, a rhythm: running stitch across the seam where two cloths are joined — the mend is allowed to show. A real repeating pattern, with one clay stitch in a different thread, off-centre.

Best small-size behaviour in the set. Still reads at 32×8 as a dotted rule, which is exactly its intended use as a divider.

G — Overprint

Two hand-torn forms printed slightly out of register, only the overlap printing forest. Riso grain and a clay-light registration ghost, bleeding off all four edges.

The agent's own verdict, and I agree: weakest of the five. Two overlapping blobs is generic and the texture is doing all the persuading. Won't survive one-colour print or embroidery.

H — The Cutting

Figurative and hand-drawn: a money-plant cutting rooting in a glass of water. Everything keeping it alive happens below the waterline where nobody is looking, and it takes weeks.
