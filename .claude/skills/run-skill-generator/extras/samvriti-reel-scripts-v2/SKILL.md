# Samvriti.Space Reel Scripting Skill

This skill captures the exact workflow for writing high-performing Instagram reel content for Priyanka Varma — a psychologist building her therapy practice (@samvriti.space) for an Indian audience. Follow this process for every reel request; do not skip the interview step even if the user seems impatient — it is what makes the content not sound AI-generated.

## Core Identity & Non-Negotiables

- **Audience**: Indian, general mental health community (not just psychology students), broad emotional maturity range
- **Tone**: Soft, gentle, raw, grounded — never "overconfident influencer" voice, never generic AI-sounding language
- **Brand**: Healing • Attachment • Real life psychology. Bio: "Psychologist who talks about the things nobody taught us in school"
- **Never** use bullet-point-perfect, symmetrical "Person A vs Person B" list structures in scripts — real scripts have texture, messiness, asymmetry
- **Never** lead a hook with a clinical/psychology term (e.g. "fawn response", "cognitive dissonance") — lead with the *feeling or behaviour*, reveal the term mid-script as a reward for watching
- **Always** end caption with a soft therapy-promotion CTA: "Link in bio if this resonates 🤍" — never salesy

## The Mandatory Workflow (follow every time)

### Step 1 — Check for repetition FIRST
Before writing anything, search past conversations for topics already covered. Maintain awareness of this running list (update mentally as you go):

**Already covered (do not repeat the same angle):**
Panic attacks, can't calm down, burnout, anger as information (recorded Oct 2026), why friend can't be therapist, fawn response, cognitive dissonance in relationships, rumination/overthinking, emotional permanence, good child grows up, confirmation bias, trauma bonding, childhood conflict avoidance, anger suppression, the identified patient, mother wound, high functioning anxiety, emotional labour, anxious-avoidant dynamic, self-sabotage/repetition compulsion, why attracted to emotionally unavailable people, "need space vs I'm done", client quote format (multiple).

If unsure, use `conversation_search` with relevant keywords before proposing a new topic.

### Step 2 — Interview the user before writing (CRITICAL — do not skip)
The single biggest factor separating a good script from a "generic AI-generated" one is a real, specific, lived detail from Priyanka. Before drafting any script, ask one of these (pick whichever fits the topic):

- "From your own experience or sessions — what does this actually look like in real life? Give me one specific behaviour or sentence."
- "What's the one thing people say to you most often about this?"
- "Have you personally experienced this, or seen it most in clients — and what's the specific detail?"

If the user says "I can't think of anything" or "give ideas" — offer 4-5 concrete options framed as things she might have witnessed, written in first-person/observational language, and ask her to pick the one that feels true. Do NOT proceed to the full script until she has either given a detail or picked from your options. This single step is what makes scripts feel authentic instead of templated.

### Step 3 — Research before writing
For any concept-based script (psychology theory, trauma response, attachment concept, etc.), run 1-3 web searches for the actual research/theorist/psychology literature behind it before writing. Look specifically for:
- The original theorist/researcher and year (e.g. Murray Bowen, Pete Walker, Hochschild, Festinger)
- One surprising/non-obvious research finding (this becomes the "mind-blowing moment" line)
- Physical/health connections where relevant (these get high shares)

Never present invented statistics or studies as fact — only cite what search results actually support.

### Step 4 — Write the script using the format below

## Script Format (Face on Camera)

```
[HOOK — must be 1-2 sentences max, describes a FEELING or BEHAVIOUR not a concept]

[VALIDATION/RECOGNITION — make them feel seen before explaining anything]

[THE EXPLANATION — concept introduced here, in human language, Indian-context example woven in naturally]

[THE REFRAME — what to think/do instead]

[CLOSE — short, warm, often: "Check the caption. 🤍"]
```

**Length**: Default to 60 seconds max unless user specifies otherwise. This is roughly 150-180 spoken words.

**Hook-writing rule**: Always generate 4-5 hook options and let the user pick, OR if asked to "critically analyze," explicitly call out whether the hook describes a feeling (good) vs. names a concept (weak) and rewrite accordingly. A hook that makes someone answer a question about themselves ("Have you ever...") outperforms a hook that explains.

## Script Format (Text-on-Video / Client Quote)

For "client quote" format reels — get the real quote/phrase from the user first (Step 2 is non-negotiable here), then:

```
[Client quote — appears first, big, centered]

[Therapist response — line by line, one short line per screen]

[One-line science/reframe if relevant]

[Close — short, land the emotional point]
```

For "things I refuse to say/believe" or "POV" format reels — keep to 4-6 short punchy lines max, one per screen, with a tonal shift moment in the middle ("Every single one of these was said with love. And every single one made the person feel more alone.")

## Caption Format

```
[Opening line — 1-2 sentences, sets up the real-life scenario]

[2-4 short paragraphs expanding the science/insight — paraphrase research, name theorists/years where found]

[The reframe — the "you're not X, you're Y" or similar insight]

[Soft CTA: "Link in bio if this resonates. 🤍"]
```

Caption length: default to SHORT/crisp unless user explicitly asks for longer. When in doubt, cut by a third — Priyanka has repeatedly asked to shorten captions. Lead with curiosity, not a full explanation — the goal is often to make people want to read to the end, not to over-explain.

## Critical Self-Check Before Delivering Any Script (do this proactively, especially if asked to "critically analyze")

Run through this checklist and flag issues even if not asked:

1. **Does the hook explain or does it provoke recognition?** Explaining = weak. Provoking self-recognition ("have you ever...") = strong.
2. **Is there a symmetrical/listy structure that feels like a worksheet?** (e.g., "Person who needs space does X / Person who is done does Y" in parallel bullet form) — if yes, rewrite with messier, more conversational texture.
3. **Does any line use overused therapy-Instagram phrasing?** Avoid: "nervous system" as the explanation for everything, "this isn't weakness, this is X" as a repeated crutch, "your brain/body is just trying to protect you" overused without variation.
4. **Is the Indian-specific detail genuinely specific** (e.g., "log kya kahenge," elder dynamics, family group chats) or just generically inserted?
5. **One screenshot-able line** — does the script have at least one line so precise it would make someone screenshot and send it to a friend? If not, sharpen one line until it does.
6. **Header/hook timing** — for text-on-video reels, always specify line-by-line timing (2-4 seconds per line) and explicitly tell the user NOT to put all text in one block.

## Song Suggestions

Always offer 2-3 options with a clear top pick and reasoning. Prioritize:
- Songs already used successfully on the page (re-use what's working — check past conversation for this)
- Currently trending audio (flag that trending audio gives an algorithm boost independent of content quality)
- Emotional/tonal match over lyrical match when the reel is text-heavy (instrumental or unobtrusive vocals preferred so they don't compete with on-screen text)

If the user names an audio/song that can't be verified or located, use `web_search` to find the actual title/artist before guessing.

## Comment Reply Guidance

When asked to help reply to comments:
- Validate the commenter's point genuinely before adding nuance — don't be defensive
- If a comment is a content request ("can you make a video on X"), treat it as a gift: confirm the user will make it, which also re-engages that commenter later
- Keep replies warm, never clinical-sounding

## Data Analysis Guidance

When shown Instagram analytics screenshots:
- Compare against previously reported numbers if available (growth trajectory matters more than any single snapshot)
- Identify the specific format/topic of top and bottom performers, not just praise/criticize broadly
- Distinguish between a content problem and a conversion problem (views high + followers flat = conversion/bio/grid problem, not content problem) — this distinction should always be made explicit, as Priyanka tends to assume low follower growth means bad content
- Always end with 2-3 concrete, specific actions, not generic encouragement
- Be honest, not just encouraging — Priyanka has explicitly asked for critical, social-media-manager-style honesty over cheerleading

## 2026 additions (researched October 2026)

These sit on top of everything above; where they conflict, the rules above win.

**When Priyanka has already recorded a video** — don't write a new script. Run
`/run-skill-generator` on the raw file: it transcribes word by word, checks every
word with a second transcriber, cuts several versions with motion type and
captions, and produces covers, subtitles, a carousel, Story frames and this
skill's captions. Use this skill's voice for every caption it writes.

**Fact-checking her own claims** — when a recorded script cites research, search
for it before writing the caption. If it isn't supported, don't repeat it in the
caption; tell the user, and suggest a short notice line. Never edit her words out
of her own video without asking.

**Hooks**
- The first ~3 seconds decide most drop-off. Watch the first 3 s with the sound
  off: would a stranger keep watching? If not, open on a stronger line.
- If the account has Trial reels, test two hooks on the same cut and keep the winner.

**Length** — guidance conflicts (7–15 s completes best; 30–60 s earns more saves
and shares), so from one recording ship a ~10 s teaser, two ~30 s cuts and the
full piece.

**Editing for retention** — trim pauses over ~0.4 s; change framing at each new
idea; keep captions clean and restrained (word-by-word, no bouncing); one idea
per on-screen type moment; leave her face visible between them.

**Captions** — topic words in the first line; 5–8 specific hashtags rather than
20+; end with the soft CTA; add one question that invites a one-word comment,
and pin a comment that asks it.

**Repurposing ladder (one video → a week)** — Reel A (best 30 s) → carousel of
the key lines → Reel B (the reframe) → type-only cut for LinkedIn/WhatsApp →
quote post → full piece + YouTube Shorts. Stories: poll → reel share → link sticker.

## Things to Actively Avoid

- Do not suggest posting unrelated "growth quote" or generic motivational content alongside niche content — this confuses the algorithm and audience (established user preference)
- Do not suggest reposting the same content/idea to "fix" a low-performing reel — find a genuinely new angle or topic instead
- Do not design posters/graphics with code/PIL when the user wants Claude Design or Canva to do the visual work — in that case, write a detailed content/design brief instead
- Do not over-format captions with excessive bullets/headers — keep the warm prose voice
